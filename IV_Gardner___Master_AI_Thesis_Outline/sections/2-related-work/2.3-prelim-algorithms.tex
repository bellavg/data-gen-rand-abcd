\subsection{Synthesis Algorithms}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms}

\subsubsection{Rewriting, Refactoring, and Balancing}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms:primitives}
Every script in this thesis is assembled from a small set of function-preserving local
transformations \citep{mishchenko2006rewriting}. A cut of a gate $v$ is a node set through
which every PI-to-$v$ path passes, and it is $K$-feasible when it has at most $K$ members,
so the logic between the cut and $v$ computes a Boolean function of at most $K$ variables.
Rewriting enumerates $K$-feasible cuts at each AND gate and replaces the enclosed logic
with the smallest precomputed equivalent, accepting a replacement only when sharing with
the rest of the network makes it a net gain
\citep{bjesse2004compression,mishchenko2006rewriting}. Refactoring makes the same exchange
with the MFFC of~\eqref{eq:prelim:mffc} as the unit, collapsing one cone at a time and
re-expressing it \citep{mishchenko2006scalable}. Balancing restructures AND trees under
associativity to reduce the depth $\ell(G)$ of~\eqref{eq:prelim:level} at an unchanged
function. Resubstitution re-expresses a gate in terms of gates already present in the
network, its divisors, and removes the MFFC made redundant when a valid re-expression is
found \citep{mishchenko2006scalable}.

Two constructions fixed here are read repeatedly later. The first is structural hashing
\citep{kuehlmann2002robust}. Writing
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(v) = \bigl\{\, (u, \mathrm{inv}(u, v)) : u \in \operatorname{fi}(v) \,\bigr\}
    \label{eq:prelim:strash}
\end{equation}
for the signature of an AND gate, hashing maintains on construction the invariant that
$\kappa(u) \neq \kappa(v)$ for all distinct AND gates $u$ and $v$. One-hop structural
redundancy, two gates over the same fanins with the same inversions, is therefore already
gone before any summarization runs (\ref{sec:method:summary:wl}).

The second is the line between structural and functional equivalence. Two nodes are
functionally equivalent when they agree under every assignment to the PIs,
\begin{equation}
    u \equiv v \iff b_u = b_v \text{ under every PI assignment},
    \label{eq:prelim:funceq}
\end{equation}
with $b_u$ as in~\eqref{eq:prelim:inversion}, and functionally complementary when
$b_u = \neg b_v$ throughout. A Functionally Reduced AIG (FRAIG) merges every such pair,
using simulation to refute candidates cheaply and SAT to prove the survivors
\citep{mishchenko2005fraig}. Structural hashing decides~\eqref{eq:prelim:funceq} only in
the special case $\kappa(u) = \kappa(v)$; the general case is a semantic property that no
local inspection reveals. Every summarization method in this thesis stays on the
structural side of that line, because a merge by~\eqref{eq:prelim:funceq} performs part of
the optimization the model is trained to predict
(\ref{sec:discussion:limitations:methods}).

\subsubsection{The Orchestrate Script}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms:orchestrate}
The target algorithm, Orchestrate, is an orchestrated flow in the sense
of~\citet{li2024orchestration}. A conventional script commits to one ordering of
whole-graph passes. Orchestration moves the choice into the graph: at each AND gate,
rewriting, refactoring and resubstitution are attempted and the transformation with the
best local area-delay score is committed, and the sweep repeats for a bounded number of
passes. Two properties make it the target: (i) it subsumes the orderings of the fixed
scripts built from the same primitives, so the label measures optimization potential
rather than the accident of one command sequence, and (ii) it is deterministic, so $S(G)$
is a single graph rather than a distribution over runs and ``optimizability under
Orchestrate'' is well defined as the $y(G)$ of~\ref{sec:method:data:label:definition}. The
exact invocation is recorded in~\ref{sec:apx:algorithms}.

\subsubsection{The Non-Target Scripts: Deepsyn, Syn4, and C2RS}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms:others}
Three further scripts of the synthesis system were applied during dataset generation
\citep{brayton2010abc}, and every tier-1 graph that enters training is the output of one of
them (\ref{sec:method:data:tier1}). \texttt{Deepsyn} searches: randomized restructuring passes
are applied repeatedly under a wall-clock budget and the best network encountered is
returned. Its seed was drawn afresh for every graph and never recorded, so the
\texttt{Deepsyn} portion of the corpus cannot be regenerated
(\ref{sec:discussion:limitations:reproducibility}). \texttt{Syn4} applies a fixed,
deterministic restructuring flow built from the primitives
of~\ref{sec:prelim:algorithms:primitives}, invoked with no arguments, so its behaviour is
entirely that of the tool's built-in script. \texttt{C2RS} expands to a chain of balancing,
refactoring, rewriting and resubstitution steps \citep{mishchenko2006scalable} whose
resubstitution cut size widens from $K = 6$ to $K = 12$, so successive steps reason over
progressively larger windows. The invocations are recorded in~\ref{sec:apx:algorithms}.
