\subsection{Synthesis Algorithms}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms}

\subsubsection{Rewriting, Refactoring, and Balancing}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms:primitives}
Every script in this thesis is assembled from four local, function-preserving transformations
\citep{mishchenko2006rewriting}. A cut of a gate $v$ is a node set through which every
PI-to-$v$ path passes, and it is $K$-feasible when it has at most $K$ members. Rewriting
replaces the logic inside a $K$-feasible cut with the smallest precomputed equivalent,
accepting the replacement only on a net gain
\citep{bjesse2004compression,mishchenko2006rewriting}. Refactoring makes the same exchange with
the MFFC of~\eqref{eq:prelim:mffc} as the unit, and resubstitution re-expresses a gate through
gates already present in the network, removing the MFFC left redundant
\citep{mishchenko2006scalable}. Balancing restructures AND trees under associativity to reduce
the depth $\ell(G)$ of~\eqref{eq:prelim:level} at an unchanged function.

Structural hashing carries a signature on every AND gate \citep{kuehlmann2002robust},
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(v) = \bigl\{\, (u, \mathrm{inv}(u, v)) : u \in \operatorname{fi}(v) \,\bigr\},
    \label{eq:prelim:strash}
\end{equation}
and maintains $\kappa(u) \neq \kappa(v)$ for distinct AND gates on construction, so one-hop
structural redundancy is gone before any summarization runs (\ref{sec:method:summary:wl}).

Functional equivalence is the stronger relation,
\begin{equation}
    u \equiv v \iff b_u = b_v \text{ under every PI assignment},
    \label{eq:prelim:funceq}
\end{equation}
with $b_u$ as in~\eqref{eq:prelim:inversion}. A Functionally Reduced AIG (FRAIG) merges every
such pair \citep{mishchenko2005fraig}, whereas~\eqref{eq:prelim:strash}
decides~\eqref{eq:prelim:funceq} only in the special case $\kappa(u) = \kappa(v)$. Every
summarization method here stays on the structural side of that line, because a merge
by~\eqref{eq:prelim:funceq} performs part of the optimization the model is trained to predict
(\ref{sec:discussion:limitations:methods}).

\subsubsection{Optimization Objectives: Area, Power, Delay}
\label{sec:prelim:synthesis:objectives}
Area, power and delay are the costs synthesis is judged on, and none of the three is physical
before technology mapping, so each is tracked through a structural proxy on the AIG: node count
$|\mathcal{V}(G)|$ for area and depth $\ell(G)$ of~\eqref{eq:prelim:level} for delay. The ideal
is the smallest network computing the same function,
\begin{equation}
    G^{\star} \in \arg\min_{H \,\equiv\, G} \bigl| \mathcal{V}(H) \bigr|,
    \label{eq:prelim:objective}
\end{equation}
which no practical script attains. A script instead returns some $S(G) \equiv G$ with
$|\mathcal{V}(S(G))| \leq |\mathcal{V}(G)|$. Both proxies are inherited downstream: technology
mapping binds fewer cells from a smaller network and reports a shorter critical path from a
shallower one.

\subsubsection{The Orchestrate Script}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms:orchestrate}
Orchestrate, the target algorithm, moves the choice of transformation into the graph
\citep{li2024orchestration}. At each AND gate, rewriting, refactoring and resubstitution are
attempted, the one with the best local area-delay score is committed, and the sweep repeats for
a bounded number of passes. Two properties make it the target: (i) it subsumes the orderings of
the fixed scripts built from the same primitives, so the label measures optimization potential
rather than the accident of one command sequence, and (ii) it is deterministic, so $S(G)$ is a
single graph rather than a distribution over runs and optimizability under Orchestrate is well
defined as the $y(G)$ of~\ref{sec:method:data:label:definition}. The exact invocation is
recorded in~\ref{sec:apx:algorithms}.

\subsubsection{The Non-Target Scripts: Deepsyn, Syn4, and C2RS}
\label{sec:prelim:algorithms:others}
Three further scripts of the synthesis system were applied during dataset generation
\citep{brayton2010abc}, and every tier-1 graph that enters training is the output of one of them
(\ref{sec:method:data:tier1}). Deepsyn searches: randomized restructuring passes are applied
under a wall-clock budget and the best network encountered is returned. Its seed was drawn
afresh for every graph and never recorded, so that portion of the corpus cannot be regenerated
(\ref{sec:discussion:limitations:reproducibility}). Syn4 is a fixed deterministic flow over the
same primitives. C2RS is a chain of balancing, refactoring, rewriting and resubstitution steps
\citep{mishchenko2006scalable} whose resubstitution cut widens from $K = 6$ to $K = 12$, so
successive steps reason over progressively larger windows. The invocations are recorded
in~\ref{sec:apx:algorithms}.
