\subsection{Structure-Preserving and Domain-Aware Reduction}

\subsubsection{Reduction on Directed Acyclic Graphs}
\label{sec:relwork:domain:dag}
Direction changes more than notation: DAG-native architectures read direction as
causality \citep{thost2021dagnn,zhang2019dvae}, but on the reduction side only isolated
pieces exist, such as directed Kron reduction \citep{sugiyama2023kron}. Coarsening
guarantees are stated for undirected graphs and do not carry to a DAG's quotient, which
need not itself be acyclic (\ref{sec:prelim:aig:distinct}), so every method run here is
compared against a random control at matched compression
(\ref{sec:method:summary:random}) rather than trusted on a guarantee for a different
object.

\subsubsection{Reduction in Circuit and Netlist Domains}
\label{sec:relwork:domain:circuits}
AIG-native reduction predates summarization and ignores it. Structural hashing merges
gates agreeing on the fanin signature~\eqref{eq:prelim:strash} at construction
\citep{kuehlmann2002robust}. FRAIG merges functionally equivalent gates, refuting
candidates by simulation and proving survivors by SAT \citep{mishchenko2005fraig}. Maximal
fanout-free cone decomposition~\eqref{eq:prelim:mffc} carves a network into mergeable
cones during refactoring \citep{mishchenko2006rewriting}, with single-fanout chain
contraction as its special case, bounded by the post-dominator
\citep{lengauer1979dominators} of~\ref{sec:prelim:aig:properties}. Read
through~\ref{sec:prelim:reduction:summarization}, all three are equivalence-based
coarsenings under another name, part of the contribution claimed here
(\ref{sec:prelim:gnn:expressivity}).

% hashing merges gates that agree on the fanin signature~\eqref{eq:prelim:strash} at the
% moment of construction \citep{kuehlmann2002robust}. FRAIG merges functionally equivalent
% gates, refuting candidates by simulation and proving the survivors by SAT
% \citep{mishchenko2005fraig}. Maximal fanout-free cone decomposition~\eqref{eq:prelim:mffc}
% is how refactoring already carves a network into mergeable cones
% \citep{mishchenko2006rewriting}. Contracting a chain of single-fanout gates is its

% \citep{lengauer1979dominators} bounds a cone whose interior reconverges. Read through the
% lens of~\ref{sec:prelim:reduction:summarization}, all three are equivalence-based
% coarsenings under another name, and connecting them to the exactness framework
% of~\ref{sec:prelim:gnn:expressivity} is part of the contribution claimed here.

That connection carries a hazard generic coarsening never meets: the regressed label is
the fraction of nodes synthesis removes, and the reductions above are themselves synthesis
operations, so a coarsener from that family shrinks the very quantity it should predict.
The hazard is graded: merging by functional equivalence~\eqref{eq:prelim:funceq} is what
synthesis does, by cone structure is what synthesis operates on, by an invariant the
encoder cannot see touches neither. No method here sits at the first position; only the
domain-specific candidate sits at the second, so it alone must argue intra-cone wiring is
noise with respect to the label
(\ref{sec:method:summary:domain},~\ref{sec:discussion:limitations:methods}).

CTS-Bench \citep{cts_bench2026}, the nearest published competitor, benchmarks coarsening
trade-offs for GNNs on post-placement gate-level netlists for clock-skew prediction with
one bespoke heuristic consuming physical coordinates, so it cannot reach a pre-physical
AIG (\ref{sec:prelim:synthesis:flow}) and compares no second method. Its findings support
this thesis's premise: up to $17.2\times$ memory reduction, $3\times$ training speedup,
accuracy collapsing to negative $R^2$ under zero-shot cross-state evaluation, and a call
for domain-aware coarsening. Two numbers carry forward: the negative $R^2$ warns against
assuming a model trained on reduced graphs serves full ones, and its MAE nearly unmoved at
0.16 to 0.17 while $R^2$ fell below zero motivates reporting RMSE, $R^2$ and Spearman
correlation together (\ref{sec:method:experiment:metrics}).
