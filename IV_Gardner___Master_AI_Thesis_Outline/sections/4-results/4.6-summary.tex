\subsection{Consolidated Summary}
\label{sec:results:gallery:summary}
One row per method collects retention, offline cost, memory and time saving against the
full-graph baseline, and matched-state and cross-state accuracy in one place, so that a
method's position on every axis measured in this chapter can be read without paging back
through RQ2 to RQ5.

Summarization is absent from the table below for the same reason: the family occupies its
own compression/accuracy plane here only as a placeholder, not as a measured comparison
against partitioning and sparsification.

No method leads on every axis. Random edge dropout, the naive sparsification control, holds
the best accuracy of any reduction in either evaluation state ($R^2 = 0.215$ matched, $0.247$
cross-state) at a near-zero offline cost of $0.064\,\mathrm{s}$, ahead of both domain-informed
sparsifiers. AND-gate-only returns the largest time saving, $28.9\%$, and PageRank pruning
the largest memory saving, $14.5\%$, and the best ranking score, $\rho = 0.367$, so three
different methods lead the three efficiency and ranking axes. Partitioning trails on accuracy
throughout: METIS is the single worst configuration in the table on both $R^2$ columns,
$-3.062$ matched and $-2.663$ cross-state, despite a competitive time saving. Every row above
the summarization block is measured. Every row inside it is a placeholder, and no ranking
claim in this paragraph extends to that family.
