\subsection{Matched-Compression Pairings}
\label{sec:results:rq4:pairings}
Each domain-informed method is read against one generic counterpart at the closest
compression available, since an unmatched comparison confounds the heuristic with how much
it happened to remove. Three kinds of match are used. By construction, both arms share the
same $k$ (\ref{sec:method:reduction:partitioning}). By calibration, a free parameter is tuned
to approximate the other arm's retention, as for AND-gate-only against PageRank pruning at
$\kappa = 0.8$. The third kind matches against a random control at the method's own ratio,
reserved for summarization (\ref{sec:method:summary:random}) and unpopulated until that
family is trained.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{\resultsfigs/rq4_pairing_compression}
    \caption[Pairing quality]{How closely matched each RQ4 pairing actually is. The
    spanning-forest / random-edge-dropout pair is far apart because the configured
    drop rate of 0.3 leaves 69.7\% of edges against spanning forest's 58.1\%.
    Raising the rate to approximately 0.419 would match them.}
    \label{fig:rq4_pairing_compression}
\end{figure}

The two partitioning pairings hold $k$ fixed and let retention fall out of the assignment
rule, so their match is a property of the construction rather than a tuned outcome. The
sparsification pairings need a free parameter for that, and the fourth is the one case where
the parameter was set for a different pairing entirely: the drop rate behind random edge
dropout was calibrated against AND-gate-only (\ref{sec:method:sparse:dropout}), so against
spanning forest it leaves 11.6 percentage points of edge retention on the table, and no
accuracy difference inside that pairing can be attributed to the heuristic rather than to the
gap in~\ref{fig:rq4_pairing_compression}.

Level slicing separates cleanly from random hashing, gaining $\Delta R^2 = 0.839$ and
$\Delta\rho = 0.302$ at the same $k$: a real domain-informed win, though both configurations

\subsection{Structural Interpretation}
\label{sec:results:rq4:interpretation}
A heuristic that protects the right structure should separate from its generic control even
when both arms are weak. One that does not implies the predictive signal is spread across the
AIG rather than concentrated in the fanin cones and causal chains these heuristics single out
(\ref{sec:method:reduction:partitioning},~\ref{sec:method:sparse:andgate}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \fbox{\parbox[c][0.24\textheight][c]{0.86\columnwidth}{\centering\footnotesize
    Reserved slot. No plot generated.}}
    \caption[Signal locality against protected structure]{\textbf{FABRICATED.} Reserved slot
    for the paired gap of~\ref{fig:rq4_paired_gaps} against the share of each graph's fanin
    cones and longest causal chains that a pairing's domain-informed arm specifically
    protects. A gap rising with that share would place the predictive signal inside the
    protected structure. Nothing is plotted and no data stands behind this float.}
    \label{fig:rq4_structural_locality}
\end{figure}

Level slicing is the one pairing that behaves as concentrated signal would predict: its clean
separation above (\ref{tab:rq4_pairings}) places some of the signal in topological level, the
one property it isolates cleanly (\ref{sec:method:partition:levelslice}). AND-gate-only moves
the opposite way, and span-weighted METIS's margin is inherited from a partner that collapsed
rather than earned against a competitive one. The result is split rather than uniform: one of
three unconfounded pairings supports concentrated signal, one argues against it, and the
third, spanning forest against random edge dropout, is not matched closely enough to read at
all.

\subsection{Adequacy of the Heuristics Tested}
\label{sec:results:rq4:adequacy}
These are simple adaptations, so the split result above bounds these four heuristics rather
than domain-aware reduction as an idea.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \fbox{\parbox[c][0.24\textheight][c]{0.86\columnwidth}{\centering\footnotesize
    Reserved slot. No plot generated.}}
    \caption[Heuristic scope against the wider literature]{\textbf{FABRICATED.} Reserved slot
    for the four tested heuristics placed against the wider domain-aware reduction literature
    (\ref{sec:relwork:reduction}) on the single structural signal each consults: edge span,
    topological level, gate role, or reconvergence. Multi-signal and learned heuristics are
    absent from the comparison entirely. Nothing is plotted.}
    \label{fig:rq4_heuristic_scope}
\end{figure}

Edge-span weighting, level slicing, gate-role filtering and reconvergence-bounded merging
each read exactly one structural property off the AIG and act on it with a fixed rule. None
combines signals, is learned, or is fit to the corpus. A single-signal rule separating on one
pairing and failing on another bounds what these four rules can do. It does not bound what a
more expressive domain-aware method, one that combines level, span, gate role and
reconvergence rather than picking one, could still achieve.
