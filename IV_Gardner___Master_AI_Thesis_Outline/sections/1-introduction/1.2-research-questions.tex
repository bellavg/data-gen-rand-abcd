\section{Research Questions}
\label{sec:intro:rqs}
 \emph{Optimizability} is the fraction of AIG nodes a given synthesis algorithm
removes from a circuit (formalised in~\ref{sec:prelim:formalization}
and~\ref{sec:method:data:label:definition}), and \emph{compression} is reported as the node
and edge retention ratios $\eta_{\mathcal{V}}$ and $\eta_{\mathcal{E}}$
of~\eqref{eq:prelim:retention}. Keeping the two apart is not cosmetic: several reduction
methods hold $\eta_{\mathcal{V}} = 1$ and lower $\eta_{\mathcal{E}}$ alone, so a single
compression figure is ambiguous and RQ4 depends on the definition being pinned down first.
Memory and time differences are taken per graph against the full-graph baseline and carry a
$95\%$ bootstrap interval and a two-sided paired rank test, whereas accuracy differences are
single-run point estimates (\ref{sec:method:experiment:metrics:stats}). Hypotheses are
stated for RQ3 and RQ4 alone, the two questions whose direction is predicted in advance and
can still be overturned by the result.

\subsection{RQ1: Task Feasibility and Baseline}
\label{sec:intro:rq1}
How well can a GNN predict the optimizability of a circuit directly from
its full, unreduced AIG representation, relative to trivial predictors and published
circuit-representation models?

\subsubsection{RQ1a: Protocol Sensitivity}
How much of the RQ1 result survives a stricter split? The same model is trained under three
train/test protocols differing in nothing but how graphs are assigned: a random split, a
recipe-disjoint split, and the design-disjoint split behind every result reported here
(\ref{sec:method:experiment:splitting}). The spread across the three separates structural
inference from design recognition, and the per-design breakdown under the strictest
protocol shows whether the residual error is spread evenly over held-out designs or carried
by a few (\ref{sec:results:rq1:protocol}).

\subsection{RQ2: Reduction Efficiency}
\label{sec:intro:rq2}
Applied to AIGs, how do partitioning, sparsification, and summarization/coarsening differ
in shrinkage rate, peak memory, training and inference speed, and the offline cost of
computing the reduction itself?

\subsection{RQ3: Predictive Retention and Trade-offs}
\label{sec:intro:rq3}
To what extent do models trained on reduced AIGs retain predictive accuracy (measured via
Smooth L1 Loss, RMSE, and $R^2$) and circuit-ranking ability (Spearman correlation)
relative to the full-graph baseline established in RQ1, and which techniques offer the
best accuracy-to-efficiency trade-off?

\paragraph{Hypothesis H1 (path contraction).}
At matched retention, summarization retains more accuracy than sparsification, because
contraction raises the effective receptive field of~\eqref{eq:method:receptive} where edge
deletion cannot: $\bar{c}_L(R(G)) > \bar{c}_L(G)$ under summarization and
$\bar{c}_L(R(G)) \leq \bar{c}_L(G)$ under sparsification, at the encoder depth $L$. AIGs
are deep relative to $L$, so the encoder is receptive-field starved, and over-squashing is
the failure H1 expects contraction to relieve \citep{alon2021oversquashing}.

\subsection{RQ4: Value of Domain-Informed Adaptation}
\label{sec:intro:rq4}
Do domain-informed reductions, which exploit topological level, reconvergence structure, and
gate roles, retain more predictive accuracy than their generic counterparts at matched
retention?

\paragraph{Hypothesis H2 (weak heuristics).}
The domain-informed reductions do not win. Edge-span weighting, level slicing, gate-role
filtering and reconvergence-bounded merging are simple enough that at matched
$\eta_{\mathcal{V}}$ and $\eta_{\mathcal{E}}$ they are not expected to separate from their
generic counterparts on $R^2$ or Spearman correlation. Failure to separate them places the
predictive signal across the AIG rather than in the causal cones and long paths these
heuristics protect (\ref{sec:results:rq4:adequacy}).

\subsection{RQ5: Cross-State Inference}
\label{sec:intro:rq5}
Can a model trained exclusively on reduced AIGs, whether partitioned, sparsified or
summarized, be queried on full ones at inference time without losing predictive accuracy?

A \emph{structural state} is the form in which a graph reaches the encoder, the reduced
$R(G)$ or the unreduced $G$ of~\ref{sec:prelim:formalization}, and cross-state inference
evaluates weights trained in one state on inputs in the other.

Inference is the regime where this pays off. Training stores activations and gradients for
every node in a batch, whereas forward-only inference stores neither. A model that had to be
trained on reduced graphs to fit in memory may therefore still be able to perform inference on full graphs on
substantially more modest hardware. The practical claim under test is \emph{train cheap,
infer full}. %TODO tone here wildly informal



\subsection{Scope and Delimitations}
\label{sec:intro:rqs:scope}
Optimizability prediction is the instrument, not the objective: it is the task the reductions
are compared on. The delimitations below were fixed in advance, whereas limitations found
during the work are separated out into~\ref{sec:discussion:limitations}.

\paragraph{Target synthesis algorithm.}
\label{sec:intro:scope:algorithm}
Training and evaluation target \texttt{Orchestrate} alone, since cross-algorithm variance
would not isolate what a reduction costs, leaving the \texttt{Deepsyn}, \texttt{Syn4} and
\texttt{C2RS} graphs of the same corpus unused (\ref{sec:conclusion:futurework:algorithms}).

\paragraph{Graph scale.}
\label{sec:intro:scope:scale}
The corpus is bounded at an intermediate rather than industrial scale: RQ1's full-graph
baseline has to fit in memory (\ref{sec:method:experiment:scale}), so the million- to
billion-node AIGs the motivation invokes are out of reach here
(\ref{sec:discussion:limitations:scalability}).

\paragraph{What is held fixed.}
\label{sec:intro:scope:reductions}\label{sec:intro:scope:architecture}
Partitioning, sparsification and summarization/coarsening are covered, the taxonomy of
\citet{hashemi2024survey} with partitioning substituted for the excluded condensation, which
establishes no node correspondence and so leaves RQ5 undefined
(\ref{sec:relwork:reduction:summarization}). One encoder is held fixed, an edge-aware GCN+

holds for another encoder.
