\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro:contributions}
This thesis studies graph reduction on whole AIGs. It measures what reduction costs as a
preprocessing step for GNN training, asks whether criteria built from AIG structure outperform
generic ones, and reports every result under a protocol that withholds whole designs. Six
contributions follow. (i) A corpus of AIGs extending OpenABC-D \citep{chowdhury2021openabc}
(\ref{sec:method:data}). (ii) A full-graph baseline for optimizability prediction from the AIG
alone on unseen designs, and a systematic benchmark of partitioning, sparsification, and
summarization/coarsening against it as a preprocessing step for GNN training on AIGs, measured
on node and edge retention, peak memory, throughput, the offline cost of the reduction itself,
and predictive retention. (iii) Domain-informed adaptations of each family, built from
topological level, fanout structure, and gate role, compared against their generic counterparts
at matched node and edge retention. (iv) A coarsening that is provably lossless for this
encoder \citep{bollen2023exact}, the reference point every inexact method is measured against
(\ref{sec:method:architecture:exact}), together with what losslessness demands of the encoder:
normalization, and topological level supplied either in the initial coloring or as node-level
positional information (\ref{sec:method:summary:level}). (v) An evaluation of cross-state
inference, in which a model trained on reduced AIGs is queried on full ones. (vi) A controlled
comparison of three train/test protocols: random, recipe-disjoint, and design-disjoint, ordered
by the information each admits into the test set (\ref{sec:method:experiment:splitting}). The
hypothesis under test is that the design-disjoint split alone measures structural inference,
and that the excess accuracy of the other two is design recognition.

The corpus is the enabling contribution: it draws on 55 source designs, extending OpenABC-D's 29.
The design-disjoint protocol behind the RQ1 baseline depends on that count
(\ref{sec:relwork:ml4eda:datasets}).
