\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro:contributions}

This thesis studies graph reduction on whole AIGs. It measures what reduction costs as a
preprocessing step for GNN training, asks whether AIG-specific reductions outperform
domain-agnostic ones, and reports its headline results under a protocol that withholds whole designs.
Six contributions follow. (i) A corpus of AIGs extending OpenABC-D
\citep{chowdhury2021openabc} (\ref{sec:method:data}). (ii) A full-graph baseline for
optimizability prediction from the AIG alone on unseen designs, and a systematic benchmark of
partitioning, sparsification, and summarization/coarsening against it as a preprocessing step
for GNN training on AIGs, measured on node and edge retention, peak memory, training and
inference throughput, the offline cost of the reduction itself, and predictive retention.
(iii) AIG-specific adaptations of each class, built from a
node's topological level $\ell(v)$, its fanout, or its role as a gate, compared against their
domain-agnostic counterparts at comparable node and edge retention. (iv) A coarsening derived from \citet{bollen2023exact} and
the conditions under which it is lossless for this encoder: normalization and topological level
supplied either in the initial coloring or as node-level positional information
(\ref{sec:method:architecture:exact}, \ref{sec:method:summary:level}). Where it is lossless it
is the reference point every inexact method is measured against. (v) An evaluation of cross-state
inference, in which a model trained on reduced AIGs is queried on full ones. (vi) A controlled
comparison of three train/test protocols: random, script-disjoint, and design-disjoint, ordered
by the information each admits into the test set (\ref{sec:method:experiment:splitting}). The
hypothesis under test is that the design-disjoint split alone measures structural inference,
and that the excess accuracy of the other two is script and design recognition.

The corpus is the enabling contribution: it draws on 55 source designs, the 29 of OpenABC-D
plus 26 more. Every design held out for testing is one the model cannot train on, so the number
of designs sets how large a design-disjoint test split can be
(\ref{sec:relwork:ml4eda:protocols}).
